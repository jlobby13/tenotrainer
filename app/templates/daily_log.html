Safety Check

Sudden severe pain · popping sound · inability to point your foot · inability to bear weight · visible tendon deformity — describe below to trigger an immediate safety halt.

Thompson test: lie face-down, foot hanging free — squeeze the calf. If the foot does not plantarflex, suspect a rupture and seek urgent medical assessment before continuing.

{# ── Coaching guidance ── #}
1

Aim for the full prescription — but listen to your tendon

Each exercise has a prescribed dose based on your current stage and irritability. Work through the full sets and reps where you can. Some discomfort during loading is normal and expected — tendons respond to progressive stress.

2

Pain rising above 4–5/10? Modify the set, don't push through

If pain climbs above a 4–5 out of 10 during a set, reduce your reps, take a longer rest, or lighten the load for that exercise. Do not force the full dose if symptoms escalate — log what you actually completed and record your pain scores honestly below. A modified session is always better than no session.

3

Your logs drive the plan — honest data = better decisions

Log exactly what you completed, including partial sets. If next-morning pain or stiffness is worse than usual, the engine will automatically adjust your next session. The system only works when the data reflects what actually happened.
