Functional Capacity

These tests measure how your Achilles tendon tolerates load. Complete only what is currently pain-free or within tolerable pain (≤3/10).

Single-Leg Calf Raise

On a flat surface, rise onto tiptoe on each leg separately and lower slowly. Count reps until you cannot continue due to pain or fatigue. This is the primary loading test for Achilles tendinopathy and directly informs your rehab stage and starting dosage. Recording both sides allows a Limb Symmetry Index (LSI) to be calculated — an LSI <90% indicates a meaningful strength deficit on the affected side.

Weight Bearing Lunge Test (WBLT) — optional

Stand facing a wall with your foot flat on the floor. Lunge your knee toward the wall while keeping your heel grounded. Measure the maximum distance (cm) your toe can be from the wall while still allowing the knee to touch it. The WBLT measures ankle dorsiflexion range — reduced mobility (<10 cm) increases load on the Achilles during walking and running and is a key modifiable risk factor. Recording both sides allows an LSI comparison — a side-to-side difference >10% suggests asymmetric dorsiflexion restriction that may be contributing to tendon overload.

Hopping Capacity — optional, complete only if pain ≤3/10 during activity

Hopping tests assess the Achilles tendon's ability to store and release energy under higher-speed load — a capacity required for running, jumping, and sport. These are relevant if you are targeting a return to running or sport, and are used to benchmark progress across rehab stages. Skip these if you are in acute pain or cannot walk comfortably.

Safety Check — Red Flags

If any of the following apply to you, do NOT proceed with self-managed rehabilitation. Seek immediate medical assessment.

  • Sudden popping or snapping sound at the back of the ankle
  • Complete inability to point foot downward (plantarflex)
  • Visible gap or depression in the tendon above the heel
  • Sudden severe bruising and swelling around the Achilles
